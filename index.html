    How the score works
    
      
    There is no hidden formula. Each rule below looks for one sign. If it is found, its points are added once (no matter how many times it appears). Reassuring signs take points away.
    
      
      
        
    • Raw emails are decoded first. Encoded text and HTML code are turned back into what you would actually see, so only the real message is checked.
      • 
        
    • High Risk needs a dangerous sign (marked Dangerous): a request for a password, code or payment, a risky attachment, a misleading link, or a forged or look-alike sender. Without one, the score stops at 59.
      • 
        
    • A verified sender with no dangerous sign stays Low Risk (29 or below). This is why ordinary marketing and receipts from real companies are not flagged.
      • 
        
    • A verified sender cannot cancel out danger. If a dangerous sign is present, reassuring signs do not count, because hacked accounts and scammers' own domains can pass these checks too.
